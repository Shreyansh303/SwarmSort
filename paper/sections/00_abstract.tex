\begin{abstract}
Waste sorting needs detectors that find every item in a photograph, yet much published work classifies single objects on studio backgrounds, and claims for metaheuristic hyperparameter tuning rarely include a random-search baseline or confidence intervals. We ask whether particle swarm optimization (PSO) trains a better lightweight waste detector than random search or the library defaults at an equal budget. Six training hyperparameters of a YOLOv8n detector were set by the defaults, by the best of 32 random samples, or by a swarm of 8 particles over 4 rounds, each search spending 32 short proxy trainings; a check fixed in advance confirmed that the proxy ranks settings like longer training (Spearman correlation 0.976, first generation). Each chosen setting was trained once and tested once on held-out data, with paired-bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals. The study ran in two generations: studio images, then studio plus real-world photographs, with a test-only Indian street set. On studio data, the defaults, random search and PSO tie at 0.676, 0.672 and 0.668 mean average precision at an intersection-over-union threshold of 0.5 (\mapfifty), and every difference's interval contains zero. With real-world training data, random search and PSO significantly beat the defaults on the mixed test set, by 0.017 and 0.020 \mapfifty, but not each other. On real-world photographs, all three score 0.298--0.310, with no significant differences, and on Indian photographs only 0.023--0.027. Tuning helps a little, the choice of optimizer matters little, and the data matter most. Tuning claims should include equal-budget random-search baselines and confidence intervals.
\end{abstract}
