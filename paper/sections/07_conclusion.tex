\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

SwarmSort compared a particle swarm, random search and the defaults for tuning a lightweight waste detector at an equal budget of 32 proxy trainings.

RQ1 asked whether PSO beats the other two on a held-out test set. It never meaningfully beat random search. In \genone (studio data), the arms were statistically tied (0.676, 0.672 and 0.668 \mapfifty). In \gentwo, both tuned arms beat the defaults on the mixed test set (random search by $+0.017$, PSO by $+0.020$), but PSO and random search stayed indistinguishable on the mixed, studio and real-world test sets; their only significant difference, $+0.003$ on India, lies on scores near 0.02 and is practically meaningless. RQ2 asked whether the short proxy ranks settings like longer training. It did ($\rho = 0.976$, $n = 8$, \genone only), but its small gaps between the best settings vanished after full training. RQ3 asked whether the answer changes with the domain. It does: tuning gains did not reach real-world photographs (0.298 to 0.310), and all arms failed on the Indian DWSD photographs (0.023 to 0.027), partly because of DWSD's merged boxes. Real-world training data, by contrast, raised real-world \mapfifty from 0.038--0.096 (\genone) to 0.338--0.353 (\gentwo) on the same images, at a studio cost of 0.074 to 0.105.

Two messages follow. A claim that a metaheuristic tunes a model better needs the defaults and random search at an equal budget and confidence intervals on a test split used once; without random search, our \gentwo gain for PSO would have been credited to the swarm. And for waste detection in real, especially Indian, scenes, training data matter far more than the optimizer. The next steps (Section~\ref{sec:future}) are led by Indian training data with per-item boxes and an ensemble of the three models.
